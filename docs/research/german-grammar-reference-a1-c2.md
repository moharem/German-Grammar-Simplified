# German grammar reference and proposed A1–C2 coverage

Research date: 5 October 2026. Research only: no curriculum implementation or application changes.

## Recommendation

Use **Martin Durrell, _Hammer’s German Grammar and Usage_, 7th edition (Routledge)** as the primary English editorial reference. Supplement it with **Schubert’s _A-Grammatik_, _B-Grammatik_, and _C-Grammatik_** to check learner relevance and explicit A1–C2 band coverage. Use **IDS grammis** when resolving exceptions, variation, or competing analyses. CEFR and Goethe materials should inform proficiency goals and assessment, rather than serve as the sole grammar reference.

This is a reference combination, not a recommendation to send a complete beginner through Hammer chapter by chapter. Routledge identifies its audience as intermediate/advanced students and teachers; its published contents and English descriptions establish broad grammatical scope, but do **not** establish an official A1–C2 syllabus. It distinguishes spoken/written and formal/informal use. [Routledge: description and contents](https://www.routledge.com/Hammers-German-Grammar-and-Usage/Professor-Martin-Durrell/p/book/9780367150266)

Schubert explicitly labels its three books A1/A2, B1/B2 and C1/C2. That gives a defensible coverage cross-check, not an obligatory teaching order. In particular, the A volume prioritizes practical relevance over exhaustive coverage. [A-Grammatik](https://www.schubert-verlag.de/a-grammatik.php), [B-Grammatik](https://www.schubert-verlag.de/b-grammatik.php), [C-Grammatik](https://www.schubert-verlag.de/c-grammatik.php)

For the learner, explanations should be newly written in accessible English, beginning with what a noun, verb, subject and sentence mean. Arabic localization can follow once terminology and examples are stable. Book examples, exercises and layouts are reference material; this research does not establish permission to reproduce them.

## What the authoritative sources actually establish

### CEFR is a proficiency framework

The Council of Europe says CEFR applies across languages. Language-specific Reference Level Descriptions (RLDs), produced by national teams, supply more concrete linguistic content. Therefore CEFR itself does not prescribe a single German list allocating each grammar construction to a level. Its can-do descriptors define communicative proficiency, not a book’s chapter sequence. [Council: RLD explanation](https://www.coe.int/en/web/language-policy/cefr-reference-level-descriptions), [Council: descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors)

A grammar checklist alone cannot establish A1, B2 or C2 competence: learners also need vocabulary, reception, interaction, production and appropriate use in context. The proposed map below is a **grammar strand**, not a complete language course or exam specification.

### Goethe does publish grammar-related material

Goethe’s courses and examinations use CEFR A1–C2. Its level pages describe communicative abilities. Goethe also provides a substantial **Deutsch Online A1 phrases-and-grammar document with English explanations**, organized by course chapters. This verifies beginner grammar support; it does not verify a comprehensive A1–C2 reference book. [Goethe: levels](https://www.goethe.de/ins/de/en/uun/dln/ger.html), [Goethe: English A1 phrases and grammar](https://lernen.goethe.de/deutschonline/A1/PDF/EN/deutschonline_phrases_grammar_1-18.pdf)

The crucial qualification is **_Profile deutsch_**. Goethe describes an open, flexible curriculum-planning tool with a companion book and CD-ROM database, six-level can-do descriptions, text types, vocabulary, grammar descriptions and language functions. The Council lists the 2005 Langenscheidt publication as a German RLD, initiated by Goethe and developed by a trinational team. Thus it would be wrong to say Goethe offers no grammar specification or German-specific CEFR resource. It is equally wrong to treat that resource as proof of one complete, compulsory, freely accessible grammar syllabus. [Goethe: Profile deutsch](https://www.goethe.de/de/m/spr/sbp/prd.html), [Council: developed RLDs, German entry](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/reference-level-descriptions-rlds-developed-so-far)

**Unverified:** the full database’s grammar entries, precise coverage by individual level, current purchase availability, compatibility of the legacy CD-ROM with present computers, and release of a revised online version. The Council mentions revision discussions beginning in 2018; that does not confirm publication. _Profile deutsch_ would be a useful further calibration source if an accessible copy can be inspected, but is not the practical core reference for this project today.

### Four things that should not be conflated

| Kind | What it supplies | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Teaching textbook | A selected progression, vocabulary, communication tasks and practice | All grammatical exceptions or a universal order |
| Reference / exercise grammar | Rules, usage, forms, look-up structure; sometimes exercises | A complete four-skills course or sufficient preparation for every exam |
| Exam materials | Task formats, assessment demands and examples for a particular examination | Exhaustive grammar coverage |
| Proficiency framework / RLD | Competence descriptors; an RLD can add language-specific realizations | One mandatory sequence for every learner |

These are analytical distinctions used in this report. They explain why a combination is preferable to choosing a resource only because an institution is well known.

## How source coverage and our proposed levels are separated

The inventory below is an original practical **coverage checklist and sequencing proposal**. Topic families are supported by the consulted reference contents and IDS articles. Its small-grained breakdown and every individual A1/A2/B1/B2/C1/C2 allocation are pedagogical judgments unless stated otherwise. Public contents prove that a heading is covered, not that every exception is explained in a particular chapter.

Source labels used below:

- **A / B / C:** the linked Schubert contents establish coverage in the paired A1/A2, B1/B2 or C1/C2 band, not a split within that pair.
- **H:** Hammer’s published contents establish a reference topic family; no CEFR assignment is implied.
- **IDS:** a linked institutional article supports a specific detail or analytical distinction.
- **P:** finer checklist detail, placement or learning dependency proposed here; verify the eventual lesson against the reference before authoring it.

Examples of source/order differences: A’s contents already include introductory passive, relative clauses and polite subjunctive patterns; B includes reported speech and future perfect. Our proposal can introduce a useful fixed expression early while delaying its full productive system. C revisits tense, noun forms and word order rather than consisting entirely of new constructions. This supports a spiral, not six disjoint lists. [A contents](https://www.schubert-verlag.de/pdf/a-grammatik_ihv.pdf), [B contents](https://www.schubert-verlag.de/pdf/b-grammatik_ihv.pdf), [C contents](https://www.schubert-verlag.de/pdf/c-grammatik_ihv.pdf)

## Proposed level map and order

Every row is **P**, not an official Goethe or Council syllabus. “Introduce” means begin a controlled, useful version; later rows expand range, independence and nuance. Recognizing a construction can precede producing it.

| Level | Proposed sequence within the level | What later returns add |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Sentence/word roles → personal pronouns and present tense → statements/questions and verb-second → nouns, gender, plural and articles → subject vs accusative object → basic negation/possession → everyday modals and sentence bracket → common dative/preposition patterns → simple imperative and selected past narration | Accuracy and independent use of short everyday sentences; no assumption of grammar terminology or prior German |
| **A2** | Broader dative/accusative and local/time prepositions → Perfekt and common Präteritum → reflexives and verb government → adjective endings/comparison → coordination and basic verb-final clauses → indirect questions and basic relative clauses → zu-infinitives → polite hypothetical patterns, future expression and initial passive recognition | Connect familiar ideas and explain simple reasons; expand forms without presenting all exceptions at once |
| **B1** | Full common noun-phrase patterns/genitive → narrative tense contrasts and Plusquamperfekt → relative clauses with prepositions → purpose/condition/concession → productive present Konjunktiv II → process/state passive and common passive tenses → broader valency and text cohesion | Tell coherent stories, explain choices and compare constructions after their foundations |
| **B2** | Tense use including Futur II and assumptions → past counterfactuals → passive with modals/alternatives → Konjunktiv I and reported speech → subjective/evidential modals → participles/extended modifiers → nominalization and connector/preposition transformations → verb clusters and information structure | Interpret stance, compress or expand information and choose formal vs everyday formulations |
| **C1** | Complex nominal groups → extended participial/infinitive structures → fine modal/reported-speech choices → advanced connectors and clause embedding → focus/negation/particles → nominal/verbal style and cohesive information packaging → difficult government/variation | Flexible precise expression in academic/professional contexts; contextual control of already introduced systems |
| **C2** | Integrate tense/voice/mood across complex texts → ambiguous reference/scope → marked order, ellipsis and discourse constructions → fine register and standard-variety judgments → rare but relevant morphology/government → stylistic reformulation and editing | Nuance, economy and reliable comprehension/production under complexity; not “collect every rare rule” |

There is no implication that C2 requires every archaic construction or exception. Levels describe increasingly flexible control, not a count of grammar facts. Grammar placement may change after learner observation and inspection of a full RLD or exam specification.

## Detailed deduplicated inventory

Each item has one home below. Cross-level returns develop it; related comparisons refer back instead of creating duplicate topics. Dates/numbers and word formation appear only where they affect grammatical forms or structure.

### N01–N05: nouns, cases and noun phrases

Family evidence: A/B/C contents; [IDS noun categories](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/273), [IDS gender](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr%40mm/6913), [IDS nominal phrase](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/5761). Detailed subdivision and levels: P.

- **N01 Gender — A1 → C1:** three genders; gender distinct from biological sex; dictionary learning with article; suffix and semantic tendencies; compound head determines grammatical properties. Return to variable gender, homonyms, loans and gender-neutral reference; tendencies must not be taught as exceptionless rules.
- **N02 Number — A1 → B2:** plural ending families, umlaut, zero and borrowed plurals; singular-only/mass nouns and plural-only nouns; countability and portion expressions; meaning differences between plural forms; grammatical agreement with quantities. Record exceptions lexically rather than pretend one rule predicts every plural.
- **N03 Case and noun inflection — A1 → C1:** nominative subject and predicative complement; accusative objects; dative objects; genitive possession/attributes and selected government; article/adjective/noun marking as one phrase. Dative plural -n where applicable; masculine/neuter genitive -(e)s; weak masculine n-declension; mixed forms such as Name and special Herz forms; proper-name genitives. Do not define cases only through English translations or questions.
- **N04 Noun-phrase expansion — A2 → C2:** adjective, genitive, prepositional and clause attributes; head vs modifier; prenominal/postnominal forms; restrictive vs supplementary interpretation; apposition and case agreement; measurement/quantity phrases; long nested phrases and potential attachment ambiguity. [IDS extended nominal phrases](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/1483)
- **N05 Nominalization and formation — B1 → C2:** infinitives used as nouns; adjective/participle nouns with adjective inflection; compounds and derivation where these affect gender, number, argument realization or nominal style; links between verbal action and nominal expression. Vocabulary expansion and exhaustive affix lists are outside this grammar strand.

### D01–D03: articles and determiners

Family evidence: A/B/C contents and H articles/determiners. Detailed subdivision and levels: P.

- **D01 Articles — A1 → C1:** definite, indefinite and zero article; case/number paradigms; first mention vs identifiable reference; no plural indefinite article; professions and nationalities; mass/abstract/general reference; proper/geographical names, institutional expressions and idiomatic exceptions; article-preposition contractions and when emphasis/reference prevents contraction.
- **D02 Possessive, demonstrative and interrogative determiners — A1 → B2:** mein/dein/sein/ihr/unser/euer/Ihr; owner reference vs noun agreement; dieser/jener/derjenige/derselbe; welcher/was für ein; inflection, choice and emphasis. Distinguish a determiner accompanying a noun from a pronoun replacing it.
- **D03 Quantification — A1 → C2:** numbers, viel/wenig, alle/jeder/beide, einige/manche/mehrere, kein; distributive vs collective reference; determiner/adjective/pronoun behavior; singular/plural agreement and case; numeral inflection in dates, ordinal/fraction/measure expressions; restrictions and scope interactions.

### P01–P05: pronouns and reference

Family evidence: A/B/C pronoun coverage and [IDS es/man/sich](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/1067). Detailed subdivision and levels: P.

- **P01 Personal/address — A1 → C1:** person/number/case paradigms; du/ihr/Sie; gender-based third-person reference; respectful address and capitalization; stressed vs unstressed pronouns; clear antecedents, agreement and ambiguous reference.
- **P02 Reflexive/reciprocal — A2 → B2:** mich/mir, dich/dir, sich, uns/euch; obligatory vs optional reflexivity; accusative/dative reflexives; reciprocal sich/einander and preposition combinations; distinguish genuine reflexive meaning from lexical reflexive verbs.
- **P03 Replacing/demonstrative/indefinite pronouns — A2 → C1:** ein-/kein-/possessive replacement forms; der/die/das, dieser, solcher; jemand/niemand/etwas/nichts/alles/man; case and agreement, optional endings where applicable; generic man vs personal reference; nominalized adjectives after indefinite forms.
- **P04 Interrogative/relative reference — A1 → C1:** wer/wen/wem/wessen/was; relative der/die/das and welcher; gender/number from antecedent but case from the relative clause; dessen/deren; person vs thing questions and references; clause-level was/wo compounds. Clause formation has its home in C03.
- **P05 es and pronominal adverbs — A1 → C2:** weather/impersonal patterns; es gibt; anaphoric es vs clause-referencing das; placeholder es vs required es and clause correlates; disappearance of positional es after another fronted element; da(r)-/wo(r)-preposition forms vs preposition + person pronoun; backward/forward reference and explicit correlates.

### A01–A04: adjectives, adverbs and particles

Family evidence: A/B/C adjective/adverb coverage; H modal particles; [IDS particle analysis](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/392). Detailed subdivision and levels: P.

- **A01 Adjective use/inflection — A1 → C1:** uninflected predicate/adverbial vs inflected attributive use; weak, mixed and strong endings with article, ein-word and no article; all cases/genders/numbers; multiple adjectives, quantifier-triggered endings, stem changes and indeclinable adjectives; adjectives used as nouns remain N05.
- **A02 Comparison — A2 → C1:** comparative and superlative forms; irregular roots, umlaut and spelling; als vs (so …) wie; am …-sten vs attributive superlative; degree modifiers; comparative clauses; limits on grading absolute/relational adjectives and contextual exceptions.
- **A03 Adverbs — A1 → C1:** place/direction including hin/her; time/frequency/duration; manner/reason/viewpoint/degree; interrogative adverbs; adjectival adverbs without extra suffix; gradability and positional effects; local/temporal deixis and discourse linking. Connector syntax lives in C02.
- **A04 Particles — A2 recognition → C2:** focus/additive/restrictive particles such as auch/nur/sogar/erst; degree particles; modal/attitude particles such as doch/ja/denn/wohl/eben/halt; reply/discourse functions; combinations, stress, sentence-type restrictions and scope. The same word can belong to different functional classes; avoid one-word English equivalents as complete explanations.

### V01–V05: verb forms, auxiliaries, modals and government

Evidence: [IDS verb inflection](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr%40mm/4069), [IDS stem forms](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/2769), [IDS auxiliary functions](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr%40mm/1695); B/C verb chapters. Detailed subdivision and levels: P.

- **V01 Conjugation — A1 → B2:** infinitive/stem/ending; finite vs nonfinite; person/number and subject agreement; regular, strong, mixed and irregular verbs; present stem-vowel changes; spelling/epenthetic endings; principal parts; imperative formation; sein/haben/werden/wissen and high-frequency irregular patterns.
- **V02 Prefix verbs — A1 → C1:** separable vs inseparable; prefix position in statements, subordinate clauses, infinitives and participles; ge- and zu placement; -ieren exception; variable-prefix meanings, stress link and distinctions such as separable/nonseparable uses of umfahren.
- **V03 Auxiliaries/copulas — A1 → B2:** sein/haben as lexical and auxiliary verbs; werden meaning become vs future/passive auxiliary; sein/werden/bleiben predicate complements; auxiliary choice with Perfekt; movement/change-of-state vs transitive uses and relevant regional variation. Word similarity does not imply identical function.
- **V04 Modals and related verbs — A1 → C2:** können/müssen/dürfen/sollen/wollen/mögen and möchte; ability, permission, obligation, intention, advice; must not vs need not; infinitive complement; modal use without expressed infinitive; past and hypothetical forms; subjective probability/inference and reported claim meanings of sollen/wollen; past inference via perfect infinitive; brauchen/lassen and selected perception/helping verbs. Fine probability scales are contextual, not exact percentages.
- **V05 Valency/government — A1 patterns → C2:** required complements vs optional adjuncts; transitive/intransitive; direct accusative/dative/genitive and two-object patterns; prepositional/locative and clausal complements; adjective/noun case/preposition government; reflexive valency; changed meaning with changed frame; dictionary learning as verb + construction. IDS provides dedicated valency resources, rather than a rule that every accusative means “the thing acted on.” [IDS resource inventory](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/)

### T01–T07: tense and temporal interpretation

Source-supported six-tense family: B/C contents. Auxiliaries and compound form details: [IDS auxiliary functions](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr%40mm/1695). All placements/use subdivisions: P.

- **T01 Präsens — A1 → B2:** current, habitual and general situations; present with future time reference; ongoing action without an obligatory English-style progressive; historical/narrative present later.
- **T02 Perfekt — late A1/A2 → C1:** haben/sein + participle II; regular/strong/mixed participles and prefix/-ieren rules; past-event use, current relevance and register; not a mechanical equivalent of English present perfect.
- **T03 Präteritum — A1/A2 selected forms → B2:** sein/haben/modals first; regular/strong/mixed full forms; narratives and common written uses; spoken/register/region differences; overlap with Perfekt rather than a rigid speech/writing prohibition.
- **T04 Plusquamperfekt — B1 → C1:** hatte/war + participle; event preceding a past reference; interaction with nachdem/bevor and narrative perspective; distinguish morphological tense from chronological order supplied by context.
- **T05 Futur I — A2/B1 → C1:** werden + infinitive; prediction/intention and present assumptions; contrast with present + time expression; future form does not always mean future time.
- **T06 Futur II — B2 → C2:** werden + perfect infinitive; completion relative to future reference and assumptions about past completion; auxiliary selection; low-frequency production chosen for communicative purpose.
- **T07 Integrated tense use — B1 → C2:** timelines, simultaneous/earlier/later reference; time adverbials and reported-speech relativity; consistent discourse perspective; recognition of regional/marked double-perfect forms where encountered, without requiring them as default production. No separate “progressive tense” is added to the six-tense checklist.

### W01–W03: voice

Family evidence: B/C passive coverage; specific worden/geworden distinction: [IDS auxiliary inflection](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/3654). Detail and sequencing: P.

- **W01 Process passive — A2 recognition/B1 production → C2:** werden + participle II; active object becomes nominative subject where applicable; retained dative/prepositional complements; agent omission and von/durch; present/past/perfect/pluperfect/future forms as needed; Perfekt ist … worden; impersonal passive; lexical restrictions; passive with modals, infinitives and moods.
- **W02 State/result — B1 → C2:** sein + participle; process vs resulting state; distinction from sein-Perfekt and ordinary predicate adjectives; tense/state interpretation and ambiguous analyses. Do not teach every sein + participle sequence as state passive.
- **W03 Alternatives — B2 → C2:** man constructions; sich lassen + infinitive; sein + zu; -bar adjectives; recipient bekommen/kriegen/erhalten passive where appropriate; differences in agency, possibility/necessity and register. A transformation can alter emphasis or modality, so it is not always interchangeable.

### M01–M04: mood and speech reporting

Family evidence: B/C moods; [IDS Konjunktiv](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/sgt/2233?termini=term). Detail and levels: P.

- **M01 Indicative/imperative — A1 → C1:** assertion vs directive; du/ihr/Sie/wir commands; irregular imperative, optional -e, separable verbs and reflexives; soften requests; infinitive/nominal directives; warnings and negative commands.
- **M02 Konjunktiv II — A2 formulas/B1 system → C2:** wäre/hätte/modal forms; würde + infinitive and synthetic forms; politeness, advice, wishes, potentiality and unreal conditions; present hypothetical vs past counterfactual hätte/wäre + participle; omitted wenn, irrealis comparisons and consequences; passive/modal combinations; avoid calling every würde form simply future-in-the-past.
- **M03 Konjunktiv I — B2 → C2:** present and compound reported forms; sein exceptions; form overlap with indicative; replacement by Konjunktiv II or würde where appropriate; wishes/formulas/instructions as selected receptive extensions.
- **M04 Reported speech — B1 recognition/B2 system → C2:** direct vs indirect statements/questions/commands; changes of person/deixis; relative rather than automatic English backshift; indicative/Konjunktiv I/II choices by register and interpretation; attribution and distance without claiming Konjunktiv always means disbelief.

### I01–I04: infinitives, participles and verb clusters

Evidence: B/C infinitive/participle coverage; [IDS Ersatzinfinitiv](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/1615), [IDS clause fields and cluster exceptions](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/4356). Detail and levels: P.

- **I01 Infinitives — A1 modals/A2 zu → C1:** with/without zu; separable-prefix placement; verbs/adjectives/nouns selecting infinitives; subject understood from controlling expression; zu-infinitive vs dass clause; um/ohne/(an)statt … zu and limits on subject identity; perfect and passive infinitives later.
- **I02 Participles — A2 participle II/B2 attributive system → C2:** participle I and II formation; participles in tenses vs adjective-like use; endings of attributive participles; ongoing/active vs completed/result/passive interpretations depend on verb and context, not just English “present/past” names; extended modifiers and relative-clause paraphrase.
- **I03 Participial adjuncts — B2 recognition/C1 → C2:** reduced adverbial constructions; understood subject and temporal/causal/concessive interpretation; punctuation; avoid dangling reference and stylistic over-compression.
- **I04 Verb clusters — B1 modal past/B2 system → C2:** auxiliary + infinitive/participle sequences; Ersatzinfinitiv for modals with a dependent infinitive; perception/lassen/help variants; subordinate auxiliary position exceptions; multiple auxiliaries in passive/perfect/modal combinations; distinguish default standard patterns from variants. “The finite verb is always the last word” is an unsafe universal rule.

### R01–R03: prepositions

Family evidence: A/B/C and H preposition groups. Detailed subdivision and levels: P.

- **R01 Case government — A1 selected/A2 system → C1:** accusative-only, dative-only, two-way and genitive-governing families; contractions; pronoun complements; compound and postposed/circumposed prepositions; case variation with register and particular constructions.
- **R02 Meaning contrasts — A1 → C1:** location/destination/source/path; in/an/auf/bei/zu/nach/aus/von; two-way accusative directional endpoint vs dative location, avoiding “any motion = accusative”; time points/duration/start/end using seit/vor/in/nach/bis/für and others; causal, concessive, conditional, instrumental, agent and purpose relations.
- **R03 Fixed vs productive relations — A2 → C2:** selected verb/adjective/noun prepositions vs spatial uses; person/thing questions; da-/wo- links from P05; preposition phrase vs adverbial clause paraphrase; subtle distinctions and register in advanced connectors/prepositions.

### S01–S04: sentence types, order and negation

Evidence: A/B/C sentence chapters; [IDS sentence fields](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/4356), [IDS middle-field order](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr%40mm/5261), [IDS negation/focus](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/terms/view/164). Detail and levels: P.

- **S01 Sentence types — A1 → C2:** statements, yes/no and wh questions, alternatives, commands, exclamations and wishes; verb-second/first/final patterns; grammatical form vs communicative function; echo/rhetorical questions and fragment replies later.
- **S02 Sentence bracket — A1 → C1:** finite verb in second constituent, not second word; fronted adverb/phrase and following subject; separated prefix/infinitive/participle at right bracket; subordinate verb position; fronted clauses; clusters refer to I04.
- **S03 Information and order — A2 → C2:** subject/object/pronoun order; neutral time/cause/manner/place tendencies rather than inflexible formula; given/new information, definiteness, weight, focus and stress; Vorfeld/Mittelfeld/Nachfeld; extraposition; left/right dislocation, parenthesis and ellipsis in discourse; marked order and comprehension of long sentences.
- **S04 Negation — A1 → C2:** nein/nicht/kein; nobody/nothing/never/nowhere; sentence vs contrastive focus and position; noch nicht/nicht mehr and related contrasts; nicht … sondern, weder … noch; negative questions and doch replies; interaction with quantifiers/modals and ambiguity. Do not prescribe “nicht always last” or “kein before every noun.”

### C01–C04: clause relationships and connectors

Family evidence: A/B/C complex-sentence coverage; [IDS connector dictionary](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/konnektoren), [IDS attributive clauses](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/1643). Detail and levels: P.

- **C01 Coordination — A1 → B2:** und/aber/oder/denn/sondern; coordinating sentences or constituents; shared subjects and omitted repeated material; correlative connectors such as sowohl … als auch, entweder … oder, zwar … aber; agreement and punctuation.
- **C02 Adverbial clauses/links — A2 → C2:** cause, consequence, purpose, condition, concession, contrast, time, manner, instrument and comparison; weil/denn/deshalb class contrasts; wenn vs als and wann; bevor/nachdem/während/seitdem/bis/sobald/solange; obwohl/trotzdem; damit vs um … zu; sodass vs so … dass; indem/ohne dass/als ob; proportional je … desto; unless/except constructions; concessive wh-ever and rare formal connectors selectively. Match logical relation, word order and register together.
- **C03 Content, question and relative clauses — A2 → C2:** dass and ob; embedded wh questions; noun/verb/adjective-selected clauses and correlates; relative clauses with case/prepositions/dessen/deren; free relatives with wer/was and corresponding pronouns; relative adverbs and clause-reference; embedded reported V2/verb-first constructions; nesting and reference ambiguity. Relative pronoun forms remain P04.
- **C04 Compression/expansion — B1 → C2:** finite clause ↔ infinitive where subject control permits; relative clause ↔ participial modifier; adverbial clause ↔ preposition + nominalization; verbal vs nominal style; meaning, agency and time lost or added by transformation. Connections to N05/I02/R03 are deliberate reuse, not new duplicate grammar topics.

### X01–X02: cohesion, register and adjacent boundaries

Evidence: IDS’s [communicative-functional remit](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik), C’s [stylistic transformations](https://www.schubert-verlag.de/c-grammatik.php), and [IDS corpus-variation resources](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/). Specific learning goals/levels: P.

- **X01 Cohesion — A2 → C2:** track who/what pronouns, demonstratives and pronominal adverbs refer to; article choice across discourse; tense continuity and logical links; topic progression; ellipsis and substitution; resolve multiple possible antecedents; control referential economy without making texts unclear.
- **X02 Register/variation — B1 awareness → C2:** everyday vs formal written grammar; academic/professional nominal style; spoken fragments, dislocation and selected colloquial constructions; national/regional standard variants and marked/nonstandard distinctions; avoid treating every corpus occurrence as recommended production; editing for precision, stance and naturalness.

**Adjacent topics:** include noun capitalization, nominalization spelling, clause/infinitive commas and stress only when they signal grammatical structure or distinguish constructions. Numerals/calendar expressions matter where inflection or prepositions change. Pronunciation training, general spelling, extensive vocabulary, idioms, cultural knowledge and specialized literary/historical grammar belong to parallel strands. No assumption of English-like aspect or a full Arabic–German contrastive grammar is made.

## Foundations before comparison lessons

This is a sequencing/design proposal responding to the trial feedback, not a new lesson or an empirically validated session-duration claim.

For a true beginner, first explain one construction’s meaning, parts and purpose with known words. Then show its form and a worked example. Let the learner inspect and rehearse it before asking an independent question. A visual card does not establish that the learner understands the prerequisites. A reasonable later pilot is 5–10 minutes with one new idea, optional repeat/explanation, and no timer; that duration is a product hypothesis to test.

| Later comparison | Prerequisites to learn separately | What a comparison must show |
|---|---|---|
| werden / würde(n) / worden / geworden | Subject agreement, finite vs infinitive/participle, sein/haben, sentence bracket; become; Perfekt; future; active/passive; Konjunktiv II | Whole construction, what changes in form, time/mood/voice, and intended meaning; they are not four interchangeable words |
| Perfekt / Präteritum / Plusquamperfekt | Past-event vocabulary, participles and auxiliary choice; each tense’s form | Same event in a timeline and actual register/context; earlier-than-past reference vs another way to narrate past |
| Accusative / dative | Noun phrases, case marking and separate verb/preposition frames | The governing verb/preposition and role, then visible ending changes; no guessing from English alone |
| weil / denn / deshalb | Complete simple statements, main-clause order and a learned subordinate clause | Same causal relation with different connector classes and verb positions |
| State/process passive, zu/dass, als/wenn/wann | Each individual construction and the required case, subject-control or time distinction | A concrete reason to select one, and when both are possible with a different emphasis |

Three brief original illustration sets:

1. **Case through the verb:** _Ich sehe den Mann._ “I see the man.” / _Ich helfe dem Mann._ “I help the man.” The noun refers to the same person; sehen selects accusative and helfen dative. Learn those frames before asking the learner to choose den/dem.
2. **Different roles of werden:** _Ich werde müde._ “I am getting tired.” / _Ich würde schlafen._ “I would sleep.” Teach becoming and hypothetical action separately before placing them side by side.
3. **Two perfect constructions:** _Er ist müde geworden._ “He has become tired.” / _Er ist geweckt worden._ “He has been woken up.” The first expresses a change of state; the second is perfect process passive. The passive auxiliary uses worden, while become uses geworden. [IDS auxiliary inflection](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/3654)

These illustrate why the old trial belongs after substantial foundations, not at the beginning. Later comparisons should hold vocabulary/context constant, highlight only changed pieces, state the use and reason in English, and offer guided explanation before retrieval. Arabic localization should preserve those semantic distinctions rather than simply replace grammatical labels word for word.

## Limits and next decisions

This is practical comprehensive coverage for a learner grammar strand, not an inventory of every German phenomenon or lexical exception. Public TOCs, descriptions and selected institutional articles were inspected; complete commercial books/databases were not read. Detailed lesson authoring still needs page-level checks against accessible copies. Some advanced entries are proposed extensions supported by broader reference families, not verified features of every candidate book. Individual level splits, time estimates and learning efficacy remain proposals.

Before implementation, decide:

1. **Reference policy:** adopt Hammer + Schubert A/B/C + IDS, or prefer Schubert as the primary sequence with Hammer as the English explanation authority. Decide whether obtaining accessible copies and inspecting Profile deutsch is worthwhile.
2. **Course identity:** grammar from zero with everyday communication contexts, or an exam-oriented grammar supplement. The present recommendation is the former; exam alignment can be added independently.
3. **First pilot boundary:** select a tiny A1 foundation such as people + sein + statements/questions; agree that comparisons and independent questions follow demonstrated understanding. Do not revive the mixed werden trial as the first lesson.
4. **Experience:** English first; short adjustable sessions; worked examples and guided practice; optional review; when to add Arabic and native-speaking review of translations.
5. **Map governance:** accept this as an editable coverage checklist, select first-introduction vs later-return goals, and validate level placement with actual learner performance and any accessible RLD evidence.

No curriculum, lesson implementation, package installation, purchase or application modification was performed for this report.
