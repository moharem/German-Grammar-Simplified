# Research for the German refresher trial

Researched 3 October 2026. Scope: one short lesson on **werden / würden / worden** for an adult returning after previously reaching approximately B1/B2. This report supports a prototype; it does not establish a universally best language-learning method.

## Experimental evidence and its limits

| Finding from a primary source                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Limit                                                                                                                                                                                 | Consequence for the trial (design inference)                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Repeated retrieval after an initial correct response improved delayed foreign-vocabulary recall; additional study alone did not in this experiment. [Karpicke & Roediger, 2008, original article](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408)                                                                                                                                                                          | Vocabulary pairs and a delayed recall task do not establish conversational fluency or German grammar transfer.                                                                        | Ask the learner to retrieve forms again in a new sentence; do not retire a concept after one correct click.                                                     |
| In a 258-participant Luganda–English vocabulary experiment, providing the correct answer after errors improved retention at one week. [Pashler et al., 2005, author-hosted article](https://laplab.ucsd.edu/articles/Pashler_etal_JEP.LMC2005.pdf)                                                                                                                                                                  | This supports supplying the answer, not a particular style of grammar explanation or universally optimal feedback timing.                                                             | Show the correct complete sentence after an attempt, plus a short explanation of the relevant contrast.                                                         |
| Three experiments found right/wrong feedback much less useful for error correction than receiving answers or reviewing the material. [Fazio et al., 2010, original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4073309/)                                                                                                                                                                                      | Materials included facts and vocabulary; this is not a direct comparison of detailed German explanations.                                                                             | Avoid feedback that ends at “incorrect”; connect the answer to meaning and structure.                                                                           |
| In a large facts-learning experiment, the best interval between study occasions depended on the intended retention delay; a longer gap was not always better. [Cepeda et al., 2008, author-hosted article](https://laplab.ucsd.edu/articles/Cepeda%20et%20al%202008_psychsci.pdf)                                                                                                                                   | This does not prescribe a validated review schedule for this person or this grammar lesson.                                                                                           | Offer a short revisit on another day. A next-day suggestion is a transparent starting default, not a scientifically optimal promise.                            |
| In 115 Japanese learners practising five English grammar structures, interleaving outperformed blocking on adjusted grammatical sensitivity at one week; immediate posttest differences were not significant. Practice was multiple choice, with answer and metalinguistic feedback. [Nakata & Suzuki, 2019, author-hosted article](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Nakata-Suzuki-2019-MLJ.pdf) | Timing/spacing and interleaving were confounded; target language, L1 and outcome differ from this trial. The study does not establish that a blocked-then-mixed schedule is superior. | Mix confusable forms after a compact explanation, while keeping examples comprehensible. Do not interpret harder initial mixed practice as evidence of failure. |
| An English hypothetical-conditional study did not find a unique benefit of output over comprehension activities; extended output opportunities with relevant input improved use, and noticing varied by task and learner. [Izumi & Bigelow, 2000, original article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2307/3587952)                                                                                        | Producing a sentence is not automatically sufficient for learning, and a scripted dialogue is not a conversation partner.                                                             | Include constrained written production and short meaningful dialogues alongside explanations. Treat speaking transfer as a future evaluation question.          |

## Verified grammar boundaries

These examples are newly written for the app. They illustrate the structures documented by the linked IDS sources; they are not quotations from those sources.

| Meaning / structure                                            | Correct trial example                                                                                                                                                                     | What feedback should explain                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **werden** as “become”                                         | **Nach dem Training werde ich müde.** — After training I get tired.                                                                                                                       | A change of state; no other lexical verb is required. The principal forms are **wird – wurde – ist geworden**. [IDS verb entry](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/verbvalenz/401217)                                                                                                                 |
| **werden + infinitive** for Futur I                            | **Ich werde die E-Mail morgen beantworten.** — I will answer the email tomorrow.                                                                                                          | The lexical verb is an infinitive. Future meaning can also use present tense: **Ich beantworte die E-Mail morgen.** Avoid claiming German future always requires _werden_. Futur I can also express a supposition. [IDS teaching grammar: Tempus](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr%40mm/6905) |
| **werden + Partizip II** for process passive                   | **Die E-Mail wird beantwortet.** — The email is being answered.                                                                                                                           | _beantwortet_ is a participle, unlike _beantworten_ in the future example. The action is foregrounded. [IDS definition of werden-passive](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/terms/view/290)                                                                                                          |
| **würde / würden + infinitive** for a Konjunktiv II paraphrase | **Mit mehr Zeit würde ich öfter Deutsch sprechen.** — With more time I would speak German more often. **Würden Sie die Frage bitte wiederholen?** — Would you please repeat the question? | Use a hypothetical situation or a polite request. _würden_ is the plural / formal-_Sie_ form, not a separate tense. _würde_ is not simply a future tense. [IDS: Würde-Formen](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/312)                                                         |
| **wurde** versus **würde**                                     | **Die Besprechung wurde verschoben.** — The meeting was postponed. **Ich würde die Besprechung verschieben.** — I would postpone the meeting.                                             | The umlaut changes the form: _wurde_ is indicative past of _werden_; _würde_ is Konjunktiv II. Teach an explicit contrast rather than treating the missing umlaut as always a harmless typo. [IDS auxiliary inflection](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/3662)                |
| Perfect process passive: **sein + Partizip II + worden**       | **Die Besprechung ist verschoben worden.** — The meeting has been postponed.                                                                                                              | The passive auxiliary’s participle is _worden_, without _ge-_. _worden_ cannot stand alone as the finite verb. [IDS passive tense table](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/systematische-grammatik/1614)                                                                                             |
| Perfect of “become”: **sein + geworden**                       | **Ich bin beim Sprechen sicherer geworden.** — I have become more confident when speaking.                                                                                                | The participle of lexical/copular _werden_ is _geworden_. Contrast **Ich bin müde geworden** with **Die E-Mail ist beantwortet worden**. [IDS auxiliary inflection](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/kontrastive-grammatik/3662)                                                                    |

## Implementable lesson decisions (inferences)

1. **Diagnostic:** three or four brief questions covering future/passive, hypothetical/past and _worden/geworden_. Call the result a practice focus, not a CEFR placement or a reliable mastery estimate.
2. **Compact explanation:** one comparison with full sentences and the changing word highlighted. Let the learner reveal English when needed. German-first and later Arabic support come from the user’s requirements, not these experiments.
3. **Mixed active practice:** combine meaning selection, typed gaps, error correction and short dialogue replies. Use work, study and training contexts. Each question must include enough context that competing grammatical answers are genuinely ruled out.
4. **Feedback:** after submission show the corrected sentence, the meaning and the structural reason. Preserve the first attempt separately from later corrections. A reveal/hint-assisted response should not count as unaided success.
5. **Bounded adaptation:** tag questions by concept; offer a new example for weak tags while retaining some mixed contrasts. Set a clear maximum so errors cannot create an endless lesson. This scheduling rule is a prototype heuristic, not a tested adaptive model.
6. **Missed-question review:** retain the missed sentences and explanations; then offer a fresh retrieval attempt. Repeating the same prompt immediately can reflect answer memory, so do not label that durable mastery.
7. **Later revisit:** persist progress locally, expose a short review button and a next-day suggestion, and make review available anytime. Validate the schedule using delayed performance rather than end-of-session scores alone.

For feedback on the trial, prioritize whether sessions feel manageable, whether explanations resolve confusion, which exercise types feel useful, and whether a later fresh example is easier. A small lesson cannot substantiate claims about restored B2 ability, fluency, or an A1–C2 curriculum.
